Current Market Condition Statistics

Inventory

Low inventory has propped up prices as rates jumped. For the first time, inventory is back above 2019, the last normal market before COVID.

Inventory is now above 2019 levels

If inventory levels rise through the spring, that will place downward pressure on prices.

Mortgage Rates

The 10-year Treasury yield drives mortgage rates. Inflation and the national debt will likely keep it elevated.

10-Year Treasury yield

Annual average, 2011–2025 (Federal Reserve via FRED). Latest: 5.00% on Oct 26, 2026.

Federal debt held by the public

$0.3 trillion in 1970. $30 trillion today. $56 trillion projected by 2036.

Trillions of dollars, fiscal year-end. Sources: OMB and Treasury via FRED; CBO projections (Feb 2026).

By historical standards, today's rates aren't high. The shock was how fast they rose from an artificially low point many buyers came to see as normal.

Home Sales

For four years, sales have hovered around 4 million a year. In more normal times, they run 5 million or more.

U.S. existing-home sales per year

Around 5.5 million a year before 2022. About 4 million today.

Source: National Association of Realtors. *2026 is the September seasonally adjusted annual pace.

That is a significant drop in transactions, driven primarily by homeowners locked in at lower rates. The market is adjusting slowly.

Existing-home sales: the monthly pace

Lowest since August 2010. October 2026: 3.75 million.

Seasonally adjusted annual rate, monthly, Jan 1999–Oct 2026. Source: National Association of Realtors.

Pending home sales in September hit their lowest level in 25 years.

Home Builders

Builder sentiment is at its lowest level since 2012.

Home builder sentiment

Only the early-1990s recession and the 2008–2011 housing bust were lower. Today: 25.

Monthly, Jan 1985–Nov 2026; above 50 means more builders see conditions as good than poor. Source: NAHB/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index.
